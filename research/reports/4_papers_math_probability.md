**Summary of A, stated skeptically.** (1) The documented, replicable edges all depend on speed or information: pre-positioning, sniping in the same block, or insider access. None of them is available to a bot reading closed 1-5 min bars. (2) The base rates are brutal: about 98-99.8% of pump.fun launches never graduate, and about 84% of graduates collapse. (3) Volume and trade-count signals are deliberately manipulated. (4) The one evidence-backed use of OHLCV and liquidity for a slow bot is **as a rug and collapse filter** (A12, A13). It is not evidence of positive alpha. Prior for any strategy found: true edge ≤ 0 after costs, so the burden of proof rests on the backtest.

## B. Statistics of strategy selection

Notation: strategy k has trade returns r_{k,1..M} (net of all costs), mean μ̂_k, standard deviation σ̂_k, per-trade Sharpe SR_k = μ̂_k/σ̂_k, t_k = SR_k·√M. Skew γ3, raw kurtosis γ4 (normal = 3).

**B1. Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (PSR).** Bailey & López de Prado 2012, "The Sharpe Ratio Efficient Frontier", J. Risk [PR] https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1821643
PSR(SR*) = Φ( (SR̂ − SR*)·√(M−1) / √(1 − γ3·SR̂ + ((γ4−1)/4)·SR̂²) ).
**Minimum track record length:** MinTRL = 1 + [1 − γ3·SR̂ + ((γ4−1)/4)·SR̂²]·(z_α/(SR̂ − SR*))². This is per-observation SR, with no annualisation needed if you work per trade. With heavy right skew (lottery payoffs), γ3 > 0 *reduces* the denominator term. But γ4 is huge (often 50-500 for memecoin trades), so MinTRL explodes.

**B2. Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR).** Bailey & López de Prado 2014, J. Portfolio Mgmt [PR] https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551
DSR = PSR(SR0), where SR0 = √(V[{SR_k}])·[(1−γ)·Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γ·Φ⁻¹(1−1/(N·e))] (γ = 0.5772 is Euler-Mascheroni, N = number of *independent* trials, and V is the variance of the SRs across trials). This is the "False Strategy Theorem": the expected maximum Sharpe ratio among N zero-skill strategies.
In t-stat units (unit variance), the expected best t among null strategies is: **N=100 → 2.53, N=300 → 2.90, N=500 → 3.05, N=1000 → 3.26**. So with 500 configurations you *expect* a t ≈ 3 winner by pure luck.

**B3. Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) via CSCV.** Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2017, J. Computational Finance [PR] https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253
Build matrix P (T time blocks × N strategies) of per-period PnL. Split rows into S equal blocks (S = 16 is typical). For each of the C(S, S/2) = 12,870 combinations: the IS rows are the chosen half and the OOS rows are the rest. Find n* = argmax IS Sharpe. Let ω̄ = the relative rank of n* in OOS, in (0,1). Compute λ = ln(ω̄/(1−ω̄)). **PBO = share of combinations with λ ≤ 0.** Also report the slope of OOS SR on IS SR (performance degradation) and P(OOS SR < 0). PBO > 0.2-0.3 means the selection process is unreliable. Python: `pypbo`, mlfinlab.

**B4. Multiple testing, Harvey & Liu.** Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016, RFS "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns" [PR] https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv059 recommends **t > 3.0** for new factors. Harvey & Liu 2015, "Backtesting", JPM [PR] https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2345489 gives the haircut Sharpe ratio:
- p_k = one-sided p-value of t_k. Adjust it: Bonferroni p_adj = min(1, N·p). Holm: sort p ascending, p_(i)·(N−i+1), enforce monotone. **BHY (FDR)**: p_(i)·N·c(N)/i with c(N) = Σ_{j=1..N} 1/j (c(500) ≈ 6.79).
- t_adj = Φ⁻¹(1 − p_adj). **Haircut SR = SR·t_adj/t.** For short, noisy samples the haircut is often 50% or more.
- Bonferroni one-sided thresholds at α = 0.05: N=100 → t > 3.29; N=300 → 3.59; **N=500 → 3.72**; N=1000 → 3.89. Bonferroni is conservative when strategies are correlated. Use effective N (B7) or the bootstrap methods (B5).

**B5. Data-snooping bootstraps.**
- White 2000, "A Reality Check for Data Snooping", Econometrica [PR] https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00152 : H0: max_k E[f_k] ≤ 0. Statistic V = max_k √M·f̄_k. Get its null distribution with Politis-Romano stationary bootstrap resamples of the *joint* PnL panel (preserving cross-strategy correlation), recentred: V*_b = max_k √M·(f̄*_{k,b} − f̄_k). p = share of V*_b ≥ V.
- Hansen 2005, "Superior Predictive Ability", JBES [PR] https://doi.org/10.1198/073500105000000063 : studentise (t_k instead of means) and recentre only strategies that are not clearly bad (f̄_k ≥ −σ̂_k·√(2 ln ln M / M)). It is less conservative when the universe contains many junk rules. Use SPA rather than RC.
- Romano & Wolf 2005, "Stepwise Multiple Testing", Econometrica [PR] https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00615.x : StepM, a stepdown with bootstrap max-t critical values. It controls FWER and **tells you which** strategies beat the benchmark, not just whether any does. This is the right tool to "pick winners". Python: `arch.bootstrap.SPA`, `StepM`.
- With concurrent trades across tokens, bootstrap **daily PnL blocks**, not individual trades. Trades on the same day share regime risk (SOL price, memecoin mania).

**B6. Walk-forward and purging.** López de Prado 2018, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, ch. 7 and 12 [book, high credibility]. Use anchored or rolling walk-forward. Purge training trades whose holding window overlaps the test window, and add an embargo (e.g. 1 day). Combinatorial purged CV gives a distribution of OOS paths. **Untouched final holdout**: the last 20-30% of calendar time, evaluated once.

**B7. Effective number of trials.** Correlated parameter-grid neighbours are not independent. Estimate N_eff by clustering the strategy return correlation matrix (López de Prado ONC; or N_eff ≈ (Σλ_i)²/Σλ_i² from the eigenvalues). **Report both raw N and N_eff, and use raw N for the final gate when in doubt.** Count *every* configuration ever tried, including abandoned ones.

**Concrete recipe (N strategies, M trades each):**
1. Freeze data, cost model and the strategy grid before looking at results. Log every run.
2. Split by **calendar time**: Train/selection 50% → Validation 20% → Final holdout 30% (the most recent). Never split tokens randomly across time.
3. On Train: compute net per-trade t_k. Run CSCV on the daily-PnL matrix and get PBO. If PBO > 0.3, stop and simplify the grid.
4. Keep candidates whose Train result passes Hansen SPA / Romano-Wolf StepM at α = 0.05 **and** DSR > 0.95 with N = raw count.
5. Re-run only the survivors on Validation (no re-tuning). Require t > 2 (one-sided p < 0.025, Bonferroni over the survivors), the same sign and magnitude, and parameter-neighbour stability (performance smooth across adjacent TP/SL values, not an isolated spike).
6. Pick at most 3-5. Run **once** on the Final holdout and report: number of trades, mean net return per trade with bootstrap 95% CI, median, win rate, max drawdown, t, PSR(0), the haircut SR, N tested, PBO, and IS-to-OOS degradation. Report failures too.
7. Then paper trade or run at minimum size live for at least MinTRL trades before scaling.

## C. Probability of TP/SL/time exits

**C1. Expected value per trade.** EV = p·W − (1−p)·L − c, where W and L are the average realised win and loss *after slippage*, and c is the round-trip cost (about 2%) plus impact. Break-even win rate: p* = (L + c)/(W + L). Example: TP +100%, SL −20%, c = 2% gives p* = 0.22/1.2 = **18.3%**. Rugs make the realised L larger than the SL: a rug gaps through the stop to about −90 to −100%. With a rug share q among losses, L_eff = (1−q)·L_SL + q·0.95.

**C2. First passage (two barriers).**
- If price is a martingale (zero drift in price), optional stopping gives E[P_τ] = P_0 for any bounded stopping rule τ, including any TP/SL/trailing/time-exit combination. **Exits cannot create edge. They only reshape the distribution** (high win rate with small wins, or low win rate with large wins). Net EV = −costs. Also, P(hit +a before −b) = b/(a+b) (arithmetic). Example: TP +100%, SL −20% gives P(TP) = 0.2/1.2 = 16.7%, exactly break-even before costs.
- Brownian motion in log price, drift ν = μ − σ²/2, barriers at ln-levels +A, −B: P(up first) = (1 − e^{−2νB/σ²}) / (1 − e^{−2ν(A+B)/σ²}). Driftless (ν = 0): B/(A+B). Expected time to exit (driftless) = A·B/σ². This gives a sanity check for the time-exit parameters.
- Edge can only come from **conditional drift**, meaning E[return | signal] ≠ 0 over the holding horizon. Test that directly: compare the signal's forward-return distribution against matched random entries on the same tokens and times. If the signal has no conditional drift, every TP/SL grid result is noise.
- Discrete monitoring and gaps: on 1-5 min bars with jumps, stops fill at gap prices. Barrier probabilities computed from continuous paths overstate how well a stop protects you.

**C3. Heavy tails.** Estimate the tail exponent of per-trade returns (upper tail of 1+r) with a Hill estimator: α̂ = [ (1/k)·Σ_{i≤k} ln(X_(i)/X_(k+1)) ]⁻¹ over the top k order statistics, or with the Clauset-Shalizi-Newman KS-based x_min selection (https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1062). If α ≤ 2, the variance is infinite, the sample Sharpe ratio and t-stat are unreliable, and the CLT converges very slowly. Use median and trimmed-mean checks and bootstrap CIs, and report results with the top 1% and top 5% of trades removed. A strategy whose EV turns negative after removing its best 1-3 trades is a lottery, not an edge.

**C4. Why lottery strategies need huge samples.** Required trades for a target t: M ≈ t²·σ²/μ². Example: p = 0.2, net win +98%, net loss −22%, so μ = +2%/trade and σ ≈ 48%. **t = 2 needs about 2,300 trades. t = 3 needs about 5,200 trades.** With N = 500 tested, you need t ≈ 3.7, i.e. about 7,900 trades. Smaller edges grow this as 1/μ². A strategy with 100-300 trades cannot be told apart from zero unless its edge is enormous.

**C5. Volatility drag.** Compound growth ≈ μ − σ²/2 per period. For a fixed fraction f of bankroll per trade, g(f) = E[ln(1 + f·r)]. A positive-mean, high-σ trade stream can still shrink the bankroll. With W = −100% outcomes (rugs) and f near 1, g → −∞.

## D. Sizing and risk

**D1. Kelly.** Binary payoff (+W with prob p, −L with prob q): f* = p/L − q/W = (pW − qL)/(WL). Continuous approximation: f* ≈ μ/σ². Example from C4: f* ≈ 0.02/0.23 ≈ **8.7% of bankroll**. At fraction k of Kelly, growth is G(k) = (2k − k²)·G* (half-Kelly keeps 75% of growth with half the variance). Betting more than 2× Kelly gives negative growth. With estimation error, μ̂ has SE σ/√M, and plugging μ̂ into Kelly overbets on average. Use the lower confidence bound of μ, or 1/4 Kelly (MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba 2010, *The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion* [book]; Thorp 2006 https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/cs286r/courses/fall12/papers/Thorpe_KellyCriterion2007.pdf).

**D2. The $20-on-$40-60 problem.** A $20 position is 33-50% of bankroll, roughly 4-6× full Kelly even for the optimistic example. That is in the negative-growth zone. Risk of ruin (diffusion approximation, Lundberg-type): RoR ≈ exp(−2·μ$·B/σ$²), with B = capital above the ruin line. Ruin here is bankroll < $20, so B = $30. With μ$ = 0.02·20 = $0.40 and σ$ = 0.48·20 = $9.6: RoR ≈ exp(−0.26) ≈ **77% even with a true +2% edge**. With zero or negative edge, RoR → 100%. Discrete gambler's ruin (±1 units): P(ruin) = ((q/p)^i − (q/p)^N)/(1 − (q/p)^N). Practical consequence: expect the live budget to be lost. Treat it as the price of out-of-sample information. Sizing it at $5 would multiply the number of live trades you can buy by 4.

**D3. Daily loss caps** do not change EV, by optional stopping. They cap variance and the speed of drawdown and stop strategy decay or bugs from draining the bankroll. Set the cap at about 2 maximum losses per day (≈ $8-10 at −20% SL plus costs). Add a kill switch: halt if the live cumulative PnL drops below the backtest's 5th-percentile bootstrap path.

## Checklist: rules for our backtest

**Data and universe**
1. Include **every** token in the universe, dead and rugged ones too. A survivor-only OHLCV source (tokens still listed) invalidates results. Forward-fill no-trade minutes as "no trade possible", not as flat prices.
2. All signal features must be computable at bar close t. Entry at **bar t+1 open** at the earliest, plus latency (at least 1 bar for a slow bot). No same-bar entry on the signal bar.
3. Split by calendar time: 50% train, 20% validation, 30% final holdout (most recent, used once). Also require a positive result in at least 2 of 3 sub-periods or regimes (e.g. 2024 mania, 2025 decline, 2026).

**Costs and fills**
4. Fixed cost: about 2% round trip (DEX fee: pump.fun bonding curve about 1.25% per side since May 2025; PumpSwap/Raydium about 0.25-0.3% per side; plus priority/Jito fees in $ that are large relative to $20). Verify the current fee docs, since sources disagree.
5. Price impact from the constant-product formula: out = y·Δx(1−fee)/(x + Δx(1−fee)), impact ≈ Δx/x, i.e. ≈ 2·size/TVL per side. Add a **slippage penalty** of at least 1× that impact for MEV and sandwiching. Reject trades where the size exceeds 1% of pool quote reserve.
6. Exits at stops: fill at min(stop, bar low after the stop, next open) with impact. On rug bars (liquidity drop > 50%), fill at the post-rug price or 0.
7. **Intrabar rule:** if TP and SL are both inside one bar, assume SL first. Trailing stops update only on closed bars, using the high of the *previous* bar. No TP fill on a wick unless the bar's volume could absorb the size.
8. Mark open positions at the **impact-adjusted realisable price**, never the last trade (see A17).

**Statistics (gates)**
9. Record N = every configuration tried. Compute expected max-t under the null: N=500 → about 3.05.
10. Train gate: Hansen SPA / Romano-Wolf p < 0.05 **and** DSR ≥ 0.95 **and** PBO (CSCV, S=16, daily PnL) ≤ 0.2.
11. **Minimum trades:** at least 1,000 trades per strategy in train and at least 300 in validation plus holdout (a lottery profile needs about 2,000-5,000; compute M ≈ t²σ²/μ² for each candidate). Prefer one trade per token per strategy. Cap the share of trades from any single token or day at 5%.
12. OOS gate (holdout): t ≥ 2 one-sided after Bonferroni over the ≤5 finalists (α = 0.025/5, so t ≥ 2.58), using a block-by-day bootstrap CI. Haircut SR reported. EV must stay > 0 after removing the top 1% of trades and after doubling the slippage.
13. Robustness: neighbouring TP/SL/time parameters (±25%) must keep the same sign. Random-entry placebo on the same tokens and times must be clearly worse. Tail exponent reported (Hill).
14. Report everything: N, N_eff, PBO, IS vs OOS SR, median and mean per trade, win rate, worst trade, max drawdown in $, the share of profit from the top 5 trades, and the failed strategies.

**Live**
15. Size so that bankroll ÷ position ≥ 10 (e.g. $5 positions on $50) and at most 1/4 Kelly from the lower CI of μ. Daily loss cap of about 2 maximum losses. Kill switch below the 5th-percentile bootstrap path. Run at least the MinTRL number of live trades before any scale-up.
16. Prior: published evidence says the base rate of positive edge for slow OHLCV rules on new memecoins is low. The most defensible design uses features as **rug and collapse filters** (A12, A13). Expect most or all candidates to fail the holdout. "None pass" is a valid, honest outcome.
